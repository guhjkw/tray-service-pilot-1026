Your screening question

Population identity when containers split and consolidate.

The answer is in the structure: population, container and the link between them are separate tables, and every change is a transaction. The demo above runs on exactly this model.

How the design keeps identity
  1. A population (PID) is the biological cohort; a container (CID) is just where some of it sits. An assignment row links them with a start and end date, so one population can be in many containers and a container's whole history is kept.
  2. A split keeps the PID. Moving part of P-26-031 into C-0102 and C-0103 adds two assignment rows. It is still one cohort, so its age, stage and history stay in one place.
  3. Consolidating two different populations creates a new PID. A lineage row links the new population to each parent with its share (by tray mass), so P-26-045 is traceable to P-26-031 and P-26-040, and through them back to P-26-027 and P-26-028.
  4. Parents are closed, never deleted. A parent with nothing left becomes Closed; one still in other trays stays Active. Recursive lineage queries give "where did this come from" and "where did it go" at any point.
  5. Every action is a transaction (service, split, consolidation, stage change) with operator and time, so the current state can always be rebuilt from the log.
